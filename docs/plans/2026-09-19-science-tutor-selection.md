# Science-tutor pilot selection and final comparison

Written before any new candidate training or inference. Supplements the campaign plan, not the completed historical comparisons.

## Pilot development selection

Use exactly the same new development prompts on the three unchanged initializations and six half/end pilot checkpoints. No final holdout, 100 STEM, judges, or existing 2,000 practical responses inform this selection.

- Freeze 64 multiple-choice development items before inference: 40 ScienceQA (eight per biology, chemistry, physics, earth/environmental and integrated-science category) and 24 SciQ. Select deterministically by SHA256 of `3407:science-dev-v1:<id>` within each bucket, after source-group/history/known-suite exclusion. Independently verify the selected keys and wording before use; flagged items are excluded and replaced deterministically before the battery seal, never after candidate responses.
- Add eight new source-grounded tutoring development cases, two per subject, from the separately reviewed heldout16 packet. Freeze all item-specific expected science, accepted alternatives, critical errors, and four science/four tutoring criteria before inference. Keep the other eight cases sealed for final evaluation.
- Keep true messages, including prior student/tutor context. No answers or rubrics enter model input. Common upstream tokenizer, deterministic greedy generation, maximum 1,024 generated tokens, full context cap 4,096; record every raw token/output and cap/failure. Identical per-item prompts and settings for all models. HF/BF16 development results are checkpoint-selection evidence, not final quantized scores.
- MC correctness and response-format adherence are separate. Unambiguous chosen option/answer content establishes correctness; competing answers or an unresolved ambiguity are incorrect. Preserve verbatim outputs and an explicit reviewed option-selection ledger. Do not award points for a correct option appearing merely in a list of rejected alternatives.
- Tutoring responses receive blinded, evidence-backed semantic review under the frozen rubric. Report science and pedagogy separately. A specified critical factual error caps the case's science score at one of four, matching the separately authored executable rubric; it does not automatically erase every pedagogy criterion. No keyword-only tutoring scores. This rule was reconciled before any candidate training/inference.

Define development selection index as 50% MC accuracy + 25% tutoring science percentage + 25% tutoring pedagogy percentage. This is a campaign decision aid, not the ADTC official score or a universal intelligence measure. Report its components beside it.

Choose one half/end checkpoint per initialization by this index; exact ties prefer higher MC correctness, then higher tutoring science, then the earlier checkpoint. At most two different initializations advance to the expanded stage, ranked by the same fixed rules. A candidate losing more than two MC items versus its unchanged initialization or introducing more critical tutoring errors is not eligible to advance. If fewer than two qualify, advance fewer; if none qualify, stop additional training and retain an unchanged control pending final matched quantized comparison. Do not tune learning rates or repeat failed evaluations based on final-suite scores.

## Expansion and final selection

The expansion must add independently reviewed/corrected science explanations and genuine tutoring interactions, not simply replicate the first pilot's short MC targets. Keep the same frozen development/final group reservations and known-prompt exclusions. Bind new sources and transformations in a separate stage manifest. Use a fresh optimizer/schedule on each exact chosen pilot checkpoint, not a second application of its historical adapter.

Freeze final contenders before any final inference. Compare selected expanded GGUFs (at most two) with unchanged upstream Qwen, original Muta, and best historical warm pilot. Identical Q4_K_M export/runtime and generation settings; preserve all raw outputs, model hashes, caps/failures, scripts, losses and checkpoint lineage. Run new source-heldout MC questions, eight fresh tutor cases, existing 2,000 practical items, 100 STEM, and judges prompts. Report source-heldout MC keys as source-keyed except individually verified audit items; do not call them all independently certified. Report the historical strict terminal-format metric separately from semantic correctness.

Final decision priority: no serious science/tutoring regression against original Muta on the known suites; no more than two lost known STEM-MC answers; higher new heldout MC accuracy and higher combined fresh-tutoring science/pedagogy, with per-subject results visible. If evidence conflicts, recommend retaining original Muta and identify the strongest challenger explicitly. Small eight-case tutoring samples are diagnostic, not sufficient for a universal-win significance claim. No retrospective composite or changed answer keys after seeing model identities. The final recommendation is not contingent on unavailable target-CPU hardware; hardware qualification remains a separately disclosed gap.
